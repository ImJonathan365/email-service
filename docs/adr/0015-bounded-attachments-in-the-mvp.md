# ADR-0015 — Bounded attachments in the MVP, transient in PostgreSQL and with no object storage

- **Status:** Rejected (owner, 2026-10-05): no product sends receipts or other attachments by email. The design is kept as a reference in case a real case appears (FR-30 stays as Won't).
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Author:** agent: Claude (documentation audit)
- **Related requirements:** FR-28 (DEPRECATED), FR-30, FR-23, NFR-10, NFR-15

## Context

The MVP excluded attachments ("they imply storage, limits and antivirus") until "a real use case" existed. That case now exists: Chinamo is a point of sale in Costa Rica and must deliver the electronic receipt to the recipient. Common practice is to email the signed XML, the tax authority's (Hacienda) response and the PDF rendition. The regulation says the receipt is "transmitted to the recipient" ([guide v4.4](https://blog.alegra.com/costa-rica/comprobantes-provisionales-y-electronicos-costa-rica/)); the exact channel is **to be confirmed** against the tax administration's current resolution. Colmena probably has the same need if it invoices.

PipeMend's monthly export does not need an attachment: a signed, expiring link is better (size, privacy, no forwarding).

Resend accepts attachments in base64 or by URL, up to 40 MB per email after encoding ([Send email](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/emails/send-email)).

## Decision

1. Attachments enter the MVP with **Should** priority (FR-30), bounded:
   - ≤ 3 files;
   - only `application/pdf`, `application/xml` and `text/xml`;
   - ≤ 2 MB decoded in total;
   - a magic-bytes check;
   - a name of ≤ 100 characters with no path separators or CR/LF.
2. **Transport:** base64 inside `POST /v1/emails` (a body limit of 3 MB when there are attachments). Remote URLs are not accepted: that avoids SSRF and removes the dependency on the link staying alive during retries.
3. **Storage:**
   - an `email_attachment` table (`bytea`) in the same transaction as the message;
   - the content is nulled when the message reaches `SENT` or a terminal state;
   - **no object storage**: at ~3,000 receipts/month of ~100 KB, the live volume is tens of MB at most, purged within seconds or minutes.
4. **Idempotency:** `request_hash` includes the SHA-256 of each attachment; attachment order is stable (determinism towards the provider).
5. **No antivirus:** products are required by contract to attach only documents they generate themselves; never files uploaded by users.
6. The batch endpoint does not accept attachments.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | For | Against | Why discarded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keeping them out of the MVP | Less code | Chinamo cannot use the service for its main case | Breaks a real case |
| Attachments by URL (the provider downloads) | No bytes in our database | URLs must live up to 23 h (retries); SSRF if we ever download ourselves; dependency on the product's storage | More fragile |
| S3/R2 for attachments | Scales to large files | A new component (violates NFR-10) with no metric to justify it | Reconsidered if the thresholds are exceeded |
| Antivirus (ClamAV) | Defence against malicious files | Another container and signatures to update; the files are generated by the product itself | Not justified with trusted producers |

## Consequences

- **Positive:** Chinamo and Colmena can integrate in Phase 0; no new infrastructure; transient data.
- **Negative / accepted trade-offs:** large temporary rows in PostgreSQL (more WAL); a 2 MB limit per email; large exports require the product to host the file.

## Review criterion

More than 10,000 emails with attachments per month, attachments > 2 MB needed, new file types, or a product needing to forward user files (→ antivirus and object storage, with an ADR of its own).
